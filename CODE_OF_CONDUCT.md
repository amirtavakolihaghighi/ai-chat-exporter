# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume they are acting in good faith. Criticise the code,
not the person who wrote it.

## What is expected

- Take the trouble to be clear. A bug report that says what you did, what you
  expected and what happened saves everyone an afternoon.
- Accept that people have different levels of experience. Someone who does not
  know what a manifest is may still have found a real bug.
- Disagree about technical decisions openly, and let them be settled by
  reasoning rather than by who is more insistent.
- Remember that most people here are doing this in their own time.

## What is not

- Personal attacks, insults, or dismissive remarks about someone's ability
- Harassment of any kind, in issues, pull requests or private messages
- Sexualised language or imagery
- Publishing anyone's private information without their permission
- Deliberately derailing discussions or wasting maintainers' time

## A note specific to this project

This tool handles people's conversations, and bug reports often include
examples. **Do not paste someone else's chat content into an issue**, and think
before pasting your own — an issue is public and permanent.

`docs/site-report.js` exists for exactly this reason: it describes how a site is
built without carrying what was said on it. Prefer it to a screenshot or an
export when reporting an extraction problem.

## If something goes wrong

Report it by opening an issue, or privately through the contact route in
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) if it is not something to discuss in public.

Reports will be read and acted on. What that means in practice: a warning for a
first lapse, and removal from the project's spaces for anything repeated or
serious. Whoever maintains the project is not obliged to host behaviour that
drives other people away.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, discussions and commit
messages — and to anyone representing the project elsewhere.
